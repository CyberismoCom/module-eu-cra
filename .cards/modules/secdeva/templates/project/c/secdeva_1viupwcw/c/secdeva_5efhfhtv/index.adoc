== Design

The Design phase ensures that security is built into your product architecture from the start. These controls run throughout active development — revisit them whenever the design evolves or new components are introduced.

.*What this phase covers*

*Context and requirements*

* *Document product security context* — Define intended use, misuse scenarios, essential functions, and environmental security assumptions.
* *Write security requirements* — Capture and review the security requirements that your product must satisfy.

*Architecture and threat analysis*

* *Manage threat models* — Create and maintain threat models to identify design weaknesses and drive security improvements.
* *Manage high-level risks* — Track broad cybersecurity risks tied to the project and product outcomes.
* *Manage interfaces* — Document exposed interfaces as potential attack surfaces.

*Technology and configuration*

* *Manage 3rd party technologies* — Assess and periodically review external and internal dependencies.
* *Make a configuration management plan* — Define how configuration items are stored, versioned, and protected.
* *Use cryptography properly and effectively* — Justify algorithm and key management choices with a dedicated review.

*Supply chain*

* *Ensure secure development in subprojects* — Verify that suppliers and subprojects follow secure development practices.
====

=== Why it matters

Fixing security weaknesses in design is far less costly than fixing them in production. Threat modeling and security requirements give the team a shared understanding of what needs protection and how — reducing surprises later in verification and operations.
